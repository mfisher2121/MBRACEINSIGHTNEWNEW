BUILDING PERFORMANCE / RESIDENT GUIDE

BEPS starts with the building.

By Max Fisher · Educational draft · sources checked 2026-10-29 · 2026-10-29

Building Energy Performance Standards, or BEPS, concern the performance of covered buildings. Start with the building and jurisdiction, then establish who is responsible for the records. A regional headline cannot establish your home’s obligations.

Maryland and Montgomery County are distinct programs

MDE identifies commercial and multifamily buildings of at least 35,000 square feet for its state program, subject to exemption procedures. Montgomery County describes its local BEPS program as covering most commercial and multifamily buildings of at least 25,000 square feet. These thresholds are a screening step, not a final determination. Maryland program ↗ · County scope and adopted regulation ↗

DC has its own program

DOEE maintains DC’s BEPS program and directs readers to its current building-energy portal. Use that jurisdiction’s records and guidance to establish coverage and applicable requirements. Start with DC DOEE ↗

If you own a condo or rent

Ask the association or building manager which building record applies, whether coverage has been confirmed, who manages benchmarking, and where the current plan and deadlines are recorded. A unit owner’s decisions and whole-building responsibilities can be different.

What a performance gap does not establish

A disclosed energy metric or a gap to a future target does not by itself prove current noncompliance, identify required HVAC work, or establish a project budget. Check reporting status, applicable deadlines, approved pathways, and official correspondence before drawing that conclusion.

A useful next conversation

Bring your building address, jurisdiction, building ID if available, current reporting record, and questions to the program administrator or your qualified adviser. This is an educational starting point, not legal advice, a compliance certification, or an engineering assessment.
