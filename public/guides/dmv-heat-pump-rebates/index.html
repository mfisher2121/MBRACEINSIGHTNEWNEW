REBATES / DC + MD + VA

A rebate headline is not an approval.

By Max Fisher · Educational draft · sources checked 2026-10-29 · 2026-10-29

A national incentive headline is a starting point. Your address, utility, household, equipment, and project timing determine which program rules you need to read. A program cap is not an award for your home.

DC: identify the program route

DOEE directs federally funded home-energy assistance through AHEP and AHRA. It describes DCSEU residential rebates as a separate route. Check the specific program before comparing offers. Read DOEE’s program explanation ↗

Maryland: distinguish a proposed launch from an open application

MEA’s September 30, 2026 update says revised federal rebate applications were submitted and launch depends on DOE approval. This does not establish access to a federal rebate today. Separate utility offers need their own review. Check MEA’s current status ↗

Virginia: use the current FAQ

Virginia Energy’s October 13, 2026 FAQ says its federal Home Energy Rebates are not yet available. Final access procedures and launch timing remain unsettled. Ask about existing alternatives through the agency’s links. Read Virginia Energy’s FAQ ↗

Rebates and tax credits are different

Do not carry old tax-credit copy into a 2026 budget. The IRS describes the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit for improvements made through December 31, 2025. That is not a promised credit for a new 2026 installation. Check the IRS timing rules ↗

Five checks before a purchase

  1. Confirm your service address and electric utility.
  2. Identify the exact program and its administrator.
  3. Ask whether preapproval, an assessment, or a participating contractor is required.
  4. Get written confirmation of qualifying equipment, timing, documentation, and payment process.
  5. Keep the dated program terms alongside your quote.

These are research steps, not an eligibility determination. Recheck the official program when making a decision; this briefing is a dated snapshot.
