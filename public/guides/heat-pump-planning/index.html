HEAT PUMPS / PLANNING

Look past the heat pump price tag.

By Max Fisher · Educational draft · sources checked 2026-10-29 · 2026-10-29

A useful heat pump conversation starts with your home and the scope of work. Keep equipment choices, home conditions, incentives, and installation responsibilities in the same record so you can compare like with like.

Describe the home before comparing prices

Record your current heating and cooling equipment, its age if known, the rooms that are uncomfortable, and the upgrades you are considering. Ask the contractor how the proposal accounts for your home’s heating and cooling needs. Keep model numbers and assumptions in the quote.

Ask what is included

Ask about the assessment, electrical and duct work, controls, permits, removal of old equipment, commissioning, warranty, and service arrangements. Identify exclusions and who is responsible for each step. These questions help define a scope; this worksheet does not size or design your system.

Resolve program requirements before relying on a discount

Some programs require an assessment or a particular contractor process. Virginia’s current FAQ, for example, describes assessment requirements in its proposed federal programs and warns against assuming a project will qualify before launch. Read the program’s own requirements ↗

Use a decision record

For each proposal, write down the cash price, separately identified potential incentive, who applies, what evidence is needed, and whether approval is confirmed or pending. Ask how a funding delay or rejection would affect your payment obligation.

Keep the next step practical

Use the Quote Companion to collect answers. Confirm the program terms with the administrator and technical scope with a qualified contractor before making a commitment. No savings, rebate eligibility, or particular equipment outcome is guaranteed by this guide.
